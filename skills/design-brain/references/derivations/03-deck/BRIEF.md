# Brief: three slides from an accessibility report

**The client is fictional.** The data on slide two is real: it is the WebAIM
Million figures the corpus cites (#1029, #710, #707, #1031, #712), the same
set used in the chart derivation. The claim about the client's own site is
part of the fiction and is labelled as such on the slide.

## The job

Three slides that open a short report meeting with a small charity, Thanet
Shoreline Trust, whose website failed an accessibility check. The slides have
to do three things in order: land the finding, show the scale of the problem,
and rank the fixes. They are presented, not sent, so they carry little text
and the argument lives in the titles (#534).

## Audience

Four trustees and a communications officer, in a meeting room, on a wall
screen about three metres from the furthest chair. None of them reads charts
for a living.

## Attributes (each with what it excludes)

- **Plain.** Excludes decoration, transitions and stock imagery.
- **Calm.** Excludes alarm colours except on the one figure that is alarming.
- **Ranked.** Excludes three equal boxes; the fixes are in order and the first
  one is bigger.

## Tone

Formal-leaning, serious, respectful, matter-of-fact.

## Content supplied

- Slide 1: the finding. The trust's home page has low-contrast text on 31 of
  its 40 text styles (fictional figure), which is the most common failure on
  the web.
- Slide 2: the WebAIM Million figures, February 2026.
- Slide 3: three fixes in order of return: contrast tokens (a day), alt text
  on the 62 images (two days), form labels on the donate form (half a day).
  Days are the consultant's estimates in the fiction.

## Constraints

- 16:9 frames that scale with the window, so they can be reviewed in a
  browser and projected.
- Everything that matters inside a 5 per cent safe area (#9147).
- Two or three type sizes across the deck (#344), sized for the back of the
  room (#881).
- No motion.
