# Playbook: brand mark and identity

Use with `core/CORE.md`. For a logo, wordmark or full identity system: the
mark, its rules, the palette, the type, and how they hold together across
touchpoints. Section 22 of the corpus is the long version.

## The job

A mark's job is to be recognised, reproduced and remembered, at every size and
in every colour it will ever be printed or displayed in. The corpus records
that only 1 of 85 participants could draw the Apple logo correctly from memory
(#832), which is a warning: marks are recognised, not recalled, so
distinctiveness and consistency do the work, not detail.

## The order of decisions

### 1. Strategy before drawing
- #820 Strategy before identity; #821 Positioning
- #822 Brand attributes: three or four, each excluding something; see
  `references/brief-translation.md`
- #823 Naming (protection rises from generic to descriptive to suggestive to
  arbitrary); #9823 Tagline; #854 Trademark and cultural checks (45 Nice
  classes; check before anyone falls in love with it)
- #841 Brand architecture: branded house, endorsed, or house of brands, decided
  before lockups exist

### 2. The mark
- Choose the type of mark for the job: #824 Wordmark, #825 Lettermark, #826
  Pictorial, #827 Abstract, #828 Emblem, #829 Combination, #830 Dynamic
- #831 Distinctiveness; #832 Memorability; #835 Appropriateness
- #74 Silhouette: it must read as a shape before it reads as a picture
- #151 Optical alignment, #152 Optical correction, #153 Optical spacing: the
  mark is drawn by eye, then checked by number
- #459 Symmetry groups and #132 Symmetry operations, if the mark uses them on
  purpose

### 3. The three tests every mark must pass
- #836 Single-colour test: it works in one colour, black on white and white on
  black
- #837 Small-size test: it holds at favicon size, 16px, and on a 24px icon grid
  (Material: 20px live area)
- #838 Clear space and minimum size: define both in units of the mark itself
  (NASA uses 1 N to 2 N of the insignia)
- Also #833 Scalability and #834 Reproducibility: embroidery, engraving,
  single-ink print, and the Android adaptive icon's 66dp safe zone inside a
  108dp layer

### 4. Colour and type of the system
- #842 Colour ownership: a colour can be owned in a category, and someone may
  already own yours
- #307 Accented neutral for the palette; #764 Colour ramp construction for the
  tints and shades the system will need; #310 Greyscale test
- #843 Typographic voice; #339 Type pairing; #341 Voice and tone of type
  (associations are learned and local; the evidence is a survey, so test on
  real headlines)
- #360 Oldstyle figures, #359 Small caps, if the voice is traditional

### 5. The rules
- #839 Misuse rules: show the wrong ways, since that is what people do
- #840 Lockups and endorsement; #838 Clear space again, on every lockup
- #844 Supporting graphic devices; #845 Photography direction; #846
  Illustration direction; #847 Iconography system (24px grid, one stroke
  weight, #490, #491)
- #848 Motion identity; #849 Sonic identity, only if the brand will actually
  move or make sound
- #850 Tone of voice as four dials per context
- #851 Brand book versus living brand system; #9851 Asset management and file
  delivery (SVG masters, named exports, one source of truth)
- #852 Touchpoint consistency
- #1056 Stretched logos and images: the most common misuse, so the rules say
  it first

### 6. Changing an existing mark
- #853 Evolution versus revolution: Gap reversed in six days in 2010; Tropicana
  in six weeks after a reported 20 per cent sales fall. Recognition is built by
  repetition and reset by redesign
- #832 Memorability; #243 Mere-exposure effect

## Checks before showing

1. Print it in black only, then white on black (#836).
2. Shrink it to 16px. Still the same shape? (#837)
3. Draw the clear space and put it next to another mark (#838).
4. Search the trademark register for the class it will trade in (#854).
5. Show it beside the three nearest competitors. Is it distinct? (#831)
6. Stretch it 10 per cent wider. Does the rules page say not to? (#1056, #839)
7. Hand the SVG master to someone else and watch them export it (#9851).

## Failures that cluster on this artefact

#1056 Stretched logos, #1039 Mixed corner radii within a system, #1040 Mixed
shadow languages, #1025 Too many typefaces, #1037 Generic imagery in the brand
